This guide was created to help those who are new to the genre of modern progressive metal, and maybe even metal in general, get started without being either overwhelmed or just not being able to find something that appeals to their taste. My approach to this guide is to help steer you in the right direction but also give you the different paths you can take to go down the rabbit hole.

The beauty of progressive metal is just how far the genre expands. It’s a broad genre with very undefined parameters, so we’re going to need to hone the interests of the listener (that’s you) so we can find what your tastes are. We’re starting off with six very different bands and moving down but also outwards, branching out into other genres of metal and rock. If you don’t like what you’re hearing, skip down to the next bullet. And if you like everything you hear, well, just keep going. Just because you don’t like the first band doesn’t mean you’ll hate all the ones in the rest of the category, so try a few before completely moving on to the next section.

WARNING: I’ve broken these bands down into categories based purely on sound. That’s the only parameter. This means instrumentation and vocal styles, not including things like lyrical content or general themes, and especially not who influenced who. This is a sonic progression through the genre, not a progression through the bands that influenced each other.

Rather than being a definitive guide, think of this as taking a quick cursory glance at the different variations of progressive metal, and picking what sticks out to you the most so you can take that further on your own journey.

Now let’s get started.

<h2>COHEED & CAMBRIA</h2>

<i>quick / poppy / punk / structured</i>

SPOTIFY PLAYLIST | YOUTUBE VIDEO

This side of prog metal is more poppy and a little bit influenced by punk. A lot of their songs have very simple structures, they move from chorus to verse to chorus like a standard song you hear on the radio, which is why I think they’re the most accessible of every band so far.
<h2>PERIPHERY</h2>

<i>djent / heavy / rhythmic / harsh</i>

SPOTIFY PLAYLIST | YOUTUBE VIDEO

This is where metal starts to get a lot more intense, rhythmic, and very djenty. The vocal styles are often influenced by more of the cores, like metalcore, and the instrumentation is a lot more distorted, almost a little robotic at times, and focus less on melody. As you will hear this sound comes from a totally different place than the previous categories, and doesn’t share much commonality with the origins and the past of progressive rock.
